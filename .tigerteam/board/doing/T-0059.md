## Goal
The minimap shows a **38×9 window** of an 80×21 map at 1:1 — under a quarter
of the level, so you never see where you are going. The user asked to *"zoom
out minimap 2x at least, so we see more of it"*. Make each minimap cell cover
a **2×2 block** of map cells so the whole level fits.

## Context
- `src/ui/minimap.ts` paints a `MINIMAP_WIDTH × MINIMAP_HEIGHT` (40×11)
  panel including a one-cell border, so the inner window is 38×9 map cells,
  centred on the hero and clamped to the map bounds.
- **The sizing, decided.** Grow the panel to **42×13** (inner **40×11**) and
  sample **2×2** map blocks, so the inner window covers **80×22 map cells** —
  the entire NetHack map (80×21) with a row to spare. The hero-centred
  scroll-and-clamp then becomes a no-op in practice, which is the point: the
  map stops moving under you.
- **Which glyph wins a block — decided, implement exactly this order.** For
  each 2×2 block take the first match, scanning the four cells:
  1. the hero;
  2. a monster (`MapCell.top` whose glyph class is a monster/pet class);
  3. an object;
  4. stairs, a fountain, an altar, a throne or a door (dungeon features
     worth navigating toward);
  5. any remembered passable terrain (floor, corridor);
  6. a wall;
  7. unexplored — a space.
  Ties inside a rank are broken by scan order (top-left first). Colour comes
  from the winning cell, unchanged.
- The hero must **always** win its block, or the player loses themselves on
  their own map — that is what rank 1 is for. Keep the inverse-video hero and
  the facing arrow exactly as they are.
- If the panel does not fit the viewport (a narrow terminal), keep the
  existing behaviour of clamping to `rect.width`/`rect.height`; at 2× a
  clamped panel simply shows less of the map.
