## Goal
The user, looking at the third-person view: *"can we make the player avatar
slightly less flat pls"*. The model is well detailed from the front but is a
slab in profile — the torso is 6.0 units wide and only 3.8 deep, so from the
diorama camera's 42° angle it reads as cardboard.

## Context
- **The sources are vendored in-repo at `vendor/afterburn/` (read-only).**
  `vendor/afterburn/src/models/pilot.js` is the reference — compare its
  torso/head/limb **depth-to-width ratios** against ours and note the
  difference in the docs.
- Current hero (`buildHeroModel`, builder units): tunic
  `6.0 w × 7.2 h × 3.8 d`, belt `6.4 × 1.3 × 4.0`, shoulder pad
  `1.9 × 1.5 × 3.2`, upper arm `1.7 × 3.4 × 3.0`, pant leg `2.6 × 11.0 × 3.0`,
  boot `2.9 × 2.3 × 3.9`.
- **The change, decided.** Bring the body's depth to roughly **0.8 × its
  width** instead of today's ~0.63, and round the silhouette so it is not a
  box:
  - torso depth `3.8 → 4.8`, belt `4.0 → 5.0`, head depth to match the torso
    within ~0.5;
  - add **chamfer boxes** at the torso's four vertical corners (a smaller
    box set in by ~0.6 on both horizontal axes, full height) so the chest
    reads as rounded rather than square — this is the trick
    `ART_BIBLE.md` §2 means by "curves are stair-stepped";
  - give the shoulders a little more depth than the chest (`+0.4`) so the
    figure has a silhouette from the side;
  - legs and arms up by the same ~25 % in depth.
- **Keep** the height at `HERO_SPRITE_HEIGHT` (0.7 cells) — this is depth
  only, the figure must not get taller or wider — and keep the box budget
  (≤ 400). Say the new box count in the docs.
- The pet may get the same treatment if it is cheap; if you leave it, say so.
