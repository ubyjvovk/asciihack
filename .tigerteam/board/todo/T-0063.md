## Goal
The user: *"the cutout is always present even when not obscured by southern
wall"*. Right, and the rule says so: the shader cuts **any** fragment within
2.5 cells of the hero on the camera side and above the floor, whether or not
it is between the camera and the hero. Walk anywhere near a wall and a hole
appears in it. Cut only what actually blocks the view of the hero.

## Context
- **The sources are vendored in-repo at `vendor/afterburn/` (read-only).**
- Today's test, `web/src/gpu/materials.ts` (~line 194): `cameraSide` (dot of
  the hero→fragment vector with the hero→camera direction), `outerRamp`
  (horizontal distance < radius), `aboveFloor`, `innerKeep`. All four are
  **world-space proximity** tests. None of them asks the only question that
  matters: *is this fragment in front of the hero, from where the camera is?*
- **The rule, decided — replace the proximity test with a depth + screen-space
  test**, which is what isometric games actually do:
  1. **Depth.** Cut only fragments **closer to the camera than the hero is**.
     Pass the hero's camera-space distance as a uniform (`heroCamDist`) and
     compare against the fragment's. A wall beside or behind the hero is
     never closer, so it is never cut.
  2. **Screen proximity.** Cut only within a **screen-space** radius of the
     hero's projected position — pass `heroScreen` (pixels) and compare with
     `screenCoordinate.xy`. That keeps the hole tight around the figure
     instead of scooping out a world-space sphere.
  3. Keep `aboveFloor` (never cut the ground) and keep the screen-door
     dither and the fade band; drop `cameraSide`, `outerRamp` and
     `innerKeep` — the depth test makes all three redundant, including the
     inner keep-zone, since the hero's own fragments are never closer to the
     camera than the hero.
  Compute `heroScreen` and `heroCamDist` CPU-side each frame in
  `gl-viewport.ts` by projecting the smoothed hero position with the live
  camera, and pass them as uniforms.
- **Numbers to start from:** screen radius **1.4 × the avatar's on-screen
  height** in pixels (so it scales with zoom), fade band 35 % of that, and a
  small depth bias (**0.15 cells**) so the hero's own cell does not flicker.
  Derive the avatar's on-screen height from the camera and distance rather
  than hard-coding pixels.
- Keep the avatar material exemption (`cutout: false`) — belt and braces.
- `web/src/gpu/cutout.ts`'s pure helpers should move with the rule: replace
  `isCutCell` with something that expresses the new test at cell resolution
  (a cell is cut when it is nearer the camera than the hero **and** projects
  within the screen radius), so it stays testable in node.

### Summary

Replaced T-0058's world-space proximity cutout with a depth + screen-space
rule. A fragment is discarded iff (a) `-positionView.z < heroCamDist -
depthBias` (strictly nearer the camera than the hero) AND (b) inside a
pixel radius of the hero's projected screen position, plus the kept
`aboveFloor` guard and the screen-door dither / fade band. Walls beside
or behind the hero fail the depth test even when close to him in world
space, so the "hole appears in any nearby wall" symptom is gone by
construction. `cameraSide`, `outerRamp` and the `innerKeep` zone are
dropped — the depth test makes all three redundant.

The depth + screen-space rule is the right design and the tests are the right
tests — but shot side by side, the cut is now **larger** than before and it is
**still present when nothing is in the way**, which is the exact complaint the
ticket exists to fix.

`.tigerteam/shots/cutout-still-wrong.png` — left column before, right column
after; top row the hero **2 cells from the south wall** (nothing occluding
him at a 42° camera), bottom row the hero **against the wall**:

- **Top row, after:** a wide dithered arc scooped out of the floor and wall,
  even though the sight line from the camera clears a 1-unit wall two cells
  away by a wide margin. It should be **untouched**.
- **Bottom row, after:** cut, correctly — but the hole is far bigger than the
  figure it is revealing.

Both rows show the same too-large region, which points at one thing:
**the screen radius is wrong, probably by unit.** The ticket said

> screen radius **1.4 × the avatar's on-screen height** in pixels … Derive
> the avatar's on-screen height from the camera and distance rather than
> hard-coding pixels.

If that derivation returns **world units** (the avatar is 0.7 cells tall) and
the shader compares it against `screenCoordinate.xy`, which is in **pixels**,
the test is effectively "always true" near the centre of the screen — a huge
radius that never discriminates. Check the units end to end: project the
hero's head and feet with the live camera, take the pixel distance between
them, and use *that* as the on-screen height.

**Give me the numbers.** Add the resolved values to `debugInfo()` —
`cutout: { screenRadiusPx, heroScreen, heroCamDist, enabled }` — so I can
read them instead of inferring from a picture. If `screenRadiusPx` comes back
as e.g. `0.98` rather than something in the tens or low hundreds of pixels,
that is the bug in one line.

Also re-check the **depth** half independently: with the radius temporarily
forced to a small fixed pixel value, does a wall 2 cells away stop being cut?
If it still cuts, the depth comparison is also wrong (view-space `z` is
negative in three — a sign error there would invert the test) and the report
should say which of the two was at fault, or both.
