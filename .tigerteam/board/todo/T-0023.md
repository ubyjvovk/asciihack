## Goal
Two things from the user's play on a wide (landscape) terminal: the
first-person view is too zoomed in — the floor tile directly in front of
the hero is below the bottom of the screen — and the floor is a uniform
brown. Fix the camera so the near floor is always visible whatever the
terminal's aspect, and make the floor a poorly-lit dark grey stone with a
flagstone texture.

## Context
- `docs/render.md`, `src/render/raycast.ts` (camera setup at the top of
  `renderFirstPerson`: `fovRad`, `vFovRad`, `fH`, `fV`, `horizon`, `posZ`;
  the floor pass; `KIND_COLORS`), `src/render/texture.ts` (add here).
- **Camera (why it is zoomed in).** Today the horizontal FOV is fixed
  (70°) and the vertical FOV is derived from the aspect
  (`70° · rows·2 / cols`), so a 278×104 terminal gets a 52° vertical FOV
  with the horizon at mid-height: the bottom row looks only 26° down and
  the nearest floor it can show is ~1 cell away — the tile in front is
  cut off. Invert it:
  - `vFovDeg` (new option, default 60) is fixed; the horizontal FOV is
    derived: `hFov = 2·atan(tan(vFov/2) · cols / (rows · cellAspect))`.
    If the caller passes `fovDeg` explicitly, keep it as the horizontal
    FOV and derive the vertical one as before (back-compat for tests).
  - `horizonFrac` (new option, default 0.42): the horizon row is
    `rows · horizonFrac` instead of `rows / 2` — the camera pitches down
    a little, more floor, less empty ceiling. Wall tops/bottoms, floor
    rows and sprite projection all use the same horizon.
  - With the defaults the bottom row looks `0.58 · 60° ≈ 35°` down, so
    the nearest visible floor is `0.5 / tan 35° ≈ 0.7` cells — the tile
    in front is on screen at every aspect. Add a test for that
    (`nearest floor row is closer than 0.8 cells at 80×24, 200×60 and
    278×104`).
- **Floor.** New colours in `KIND_COLORS`: `floor` `[0.22, 0.22, 0.24]`,
  `corridor` `[0.15, 0.14, 0.13]`, `doorway`/`door_open` floor tint
  `[0.28, 0.22, 0.14]` (keep), stairs/altar/fountain unchanged. New
  `floorShade(fX, fY)` in `texture.ts`: flagstones of side 0.5 cells,
  each stone's brightness `0.85 … 1.15` from the hash of its
  `(floor(2fX), floor(2fY))` index, thin seams (within 0.04 of a stone
  edge) at 0.6; corridors use stones of side 1.0 with no seams (rough
  rock). Keep the existing cell-edge grid but soften it to 0.85 (the
  seams now carry the perspective). Everything else (fog, lit doorways,
  stairs highlight) unchanged.
- **Readable look (the important part).** The user compared us with
  AsciiCity's amber render and asked for that readability. What makes it
  readable: dark surfaces are *sparse* (they quantize to dots or nothing),
  and only edges, silhouettes and lit details are dense. Today every wall
  face is a full-brightness block of dense glyphs, so edges drown. Rebalance
  the raycaster's brightness (all values are linear, before the quantizer's
  exposure 1.7; the quantizer is not changed):
  - Wall faces: base `0.14` (`KIND_COLORS.wall` → `[0.14, 0.14, 0.15]`,
    door_closed `[0.16, 0.11, 0.06]`, tree `[0.05, 0.14, 0.05]`, bars
    `[0.10, 0.16, 0.17]`), brick body ±0.03 from the hash, mortar lines
    `0.05` **absolute** (darker than the body, they now read as seams),
    N/S vs E/W face factor stays.
  - Edge lines are **absolute brightness**, not multiples of the base:
    wall top edge row `0.75`, bottom contact row `0.30`, corner columns
    `0.55`, doorway/door frame posts `0.70`, stairs `0.85` (already).
  - Floor: base `[0.10, 0.10, 0.11]` with the flagstone texture (stones
    ±20 %, seams `0.04` absolute); perspective grid lines at cell edges
    `0.30` absolute so they read as the converging lines; corridor base
    `[0.07, 0.065, 0.06]`.
  - Ceiling: black, no gradient (poorly lit; nothing to see up there).
  - Fog: `fogK` default `0.28` (was 0.18) so walls 8 cells away are
    already faint and the far end of a corridor fades to nothing — that
    fade *is* the depth cue in AsciiCity's frame.
  - The unknown veil and sprites are unchanged.
  Result to aim for (check it against the golden you regenerate): a wall
  3 cells away is a dark field of `.`/`:`-class glyphs with one bright
  line along its top and bright verticals at corners; the floor is a dark
  field with brighter converging lines; the ceiling is empty.
- Regenerate both goldens (camera, floor and levels changed); the
  `detail:false` golden changes too because of the camera — that is
  expected, say so in the report.
