## Goal
In the third-person view the natural thing is to click where you want to go.
Add it: click a cell, and if a walkable path through **known** terrain
exists, walk it.

## Context
- **The sources are vendored in-repo at `vendor/afterburn/` (read-only).**
- Two halves, keep them apart:
  1. **Picking** (`web/src/gpu/pick.ts`): turn a click into a map cell.
     The GL canvas has `pointer-events: none` (docs/web.md "WebGL viewport")
     so the DOM terminal keeps focus — do **not** change that. Listen on the
     existing `<pre id="term">` instead, take the event's client
     coordinates relative to the canvas rectangle, and unproject onto the
     **floor plane `y = 0`** using the viewport's live camera. Export a pure
     `cellUnderRay(origin, dir)` so the maths is testable in node without a
     renderer; the impure part reads the camera and calls it.
  2. **Travel** (`src/ui/travel.ts`): a breadth-first search over the
     session's remembered map from the hero to the target. Passable = the
     `isSolid` test already used by the renderers, and **known cells only**
     (`kindAt !== 'unexplored'`) — never path through territory the player
     has not seen. Diagonals allowed, but not squeezing between two
     diagonal walls. Returns the cell sequence, or null when unreachable.
- **Sending the moves, decided.** Convert each step to its vi-key and send
  them one at a time through the existing `sendKey` path, **one key per
  frame at most**, stopping early if: the hero does not end up where the step
  expected (something blocked or interrupted), the message line changes, or
  any key is pressed. NetHack is turn-based and can interrupt for a hundred
  reasons — a queued burst that ignores the result would walk the player into
  a monster. Do **not** use NetHack's own `_` travel command; driving its
  cursor prompt over the bridge is a bigger and more fragile job.
- A click on an unreachable or unknown cell does nothing (no beep, no
  message) — silence is the right feedback for "no path".
