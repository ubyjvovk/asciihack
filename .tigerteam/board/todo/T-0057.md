## Goal
`FpsMode` translates arrow keys into **facing-relative** moves: `Left`/`Right`
turn on the spot, `Up` walks along the facing, `Down` walks backwards. That is
right for a first-person camera and wrong for everything else. The browser now
starts in the third-person diorama view, where the user expects *"normal
nethack movement, just turn the avatar to face where he's going"*.

## Context
- Read `src/ui/modes/fps.ts` (the key handling and the 120 ms turn animation),
  `src/ui/view3d.ts` (`FACINGS`, `poseFor`, `turn`, `strafe`, `charKey`,
  `sendKey`) and `docs/ui.md` §controls.
- **The terminal client keeps today's behaviour.** `npm start`'s fps mode is a
  genuine first-person raycaster; facing-relative controls are correct there
  and must not change. This ticket adds a second scheme, it does not replace
  the first.
- **The design, decided:**
  - `FpsMode` gains a movement scheme, `'facing'` (today, the default) or
    `'absolute'`, settable at construction and at runtime via a new
    `setMovement(scheme)`.
  - In **`'absolute'`**: every direction key is passed to NetHack unchanged —
    arrows, vi-keys and numpad all mean their usual compass direction, and
    nothing is remapped, strafed or reversed. Non-direction keys are
    unaffected in both schemes.
  - The avatar's facing still exists, but it now **follows movement**: when a
    direction key is sent in absolute mode, set the facing to that compass
    direction and run the **existing** 120 ms turn animation, so the avatar
    swings round to face where it is walking instead of snapping.
  - `web/src/main.ts` picks the scheme from the live 3D view: `'facing'` when
    the GL viewport is in `fps`, `'absolute'` in `third` and `ortho`. It must
    also switch when the player presses `F2`/`F3`/`F9`, not only at load.
- `poseFor(hero, yaw)` keeps feeding the viewport; in absolute mode the yaw it
  gets is the follow-the-movement facing described above, which is exactly
  what T-0056's avatar needs to rotate to. Do not touch
  `web/src/gpu/` or `web/src/gl/` — T-0056 owns those.
