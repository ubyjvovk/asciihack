## Goal
Now that the view is third-person, the hero is the thing you look at — and
he is currently a flat tinted quad. The user: *"create a reasonable player
and pet avatars - current block is weird, maybe adopt afterburn's hero as
base"*. Build real voxel avatars for the **hero** and the **pet**, using
afterburn's pilot as the starting point.

## Context
- **The sources are vendored in-repo at `vendor/afterburn/` (read-only).**
  `~/afterburn` is not visible inside the worker container. Newly vendored
  for this ticket:
  - `vendor/afterburn/src/models/pilot.js` — **the base**: a voxel humanoid
    with head/torso/arms/legs as named parts and a walk animation.
  - `vendor/afterburn/src/models/pip.js` — their small companion robot, a
    good reference for the pet's scale and silhouette.
  - `vendor/afterburn/src/models/critters.js` — small creatures.
  - `vendor/afterburn/src/models/body.js` — their shared character rig and
    animation parameters (copied from their `src/game/`).
  - `vendor/afterburn/src/models/_demo.js` — the canonical kit example.
  Read `vendor/afterburn/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §1–2 (the model contract) and
  §2 of `ART_BIBLE.md` (voxel rules, character budget ≤ 900 boxes) first.
- **Scale.** One cell is one unit and `HERO_SPRITE_HEIGHT = 0.7` cells, so
  the hero model is **0.7 cells tall**, not afterburn's 1.7 m. Their pilot is
  authored at `unit: 0.08`; scale the builder unit so the finished model
  measures 0.7 in Y, and say in the docs what unit you used.
- **What to build** (`web/src/gpu/avatar.ts`):
  - `buildHeroModel()` — a humanoid derived from the pilot: head, torso,
    two arms, two legs, a simple adventurer's palette from
    `web/src/voxel/palette.ts` (no invented hexes). Not the pilot's orange
    flight suit — this is a NetHack adventurer, so cloth and leather tones.
  - `buildPetModel()` — a small four-legged animal at roughly 0.45 cells,
    readable as a companion from the third-person camera.
  - Both return a `VoxelModel` from the kit, so `buildModelObject` meshes
    them with the same `createVoxelMaterial` as the dungeon and they are lit
    by the same stack.
- **Wiring.** `sprites.ts` keeps billboards for everything else — monsters
  and items stay as they are. The hero (`Sprite.ch === '@'`) and the pet
  (`Sprite.cls === 'pet'`) get the voxel models instead. Build each model
  **once** and reuse the object; never rebuild per frame.
- **Facing.** Take the facing as an argument (radians, 0 = north) and rotate
  the model about Y. T-0057 is deciding where that angle comes from and owns
  `src/ui/`; this ticket must not touch `src/`. Until then, accept the
  existing `Pose.yaw` for the hero and face the pet along its last movement
  if that is cheap — otherwise face it the same way as the hero and note it.
- Budget: hero ≤ 400 boxes, pet ≤ 200 — well under the art bible's 900,
  because these are 0.7 cells tall on screen, not hero props.
