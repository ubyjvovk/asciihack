## Goal
The user: *"the cutout is always present even when not obscured by southern
wall"*. Right, and the rule says so: the shader cuts **any** fragment within
2.5 cells of the hero on the camera side and above the floor, whether or not
it is between the camera and the hero. Walk anywhere near a wall and a hole
appears in it. Cut only what actually blocks the view of the hero.

## Context
- **The sources are vendored in-repo at `vendor/afterburn/` (read-only).**
- Today's test, `web/src/gpu/materials.ts` (~line 194): `cameraSide` (dot of
  the hero→fragment vector with the hero→camera direction), `outerRamp`
  (horizontal distance < radius), `aboveFloor`, `innerKeep`. All four are
  **world-space proximity** tests. None of them asks the only question that
  matters: *is this fragment in front of the hero, from where the camera is?*
- **The rule, decided — replace the proximity test with a depth + screen-space
  test**, which is what isometric games actually do:
  1. **Depth.** Cut only fragments **closer to the camera than the hero is**.
     Pass the hero's camera-space distance as a uniform (`heroCamDist`) and
     compare against the fragment's. A wall beside or behind the hero is
     never closer, so it is never cut.
  2. **Screen proximity.** Cut only within a **screen-space** radius of the
     hero's projected position — pass `heroScreen` (pixels) and compare with
     `screenCoordinate.xy`. That keeps the hole tight around the figure
     instead of scooping out a world-space sphere.
  3. Keep `aboveFloor` (never cut the ground) and keep the screen-door
     dither and the fade band; drop `cameraSide`, `outerRamp` and
     `innerKeep` — the depth test makes all three redundant, including the
     inner keep-zone, since the hero's own fragments are never closer to the
     camera than the hero.
  Compute `heroScreen` and `heroCamDist` CPU-side each frame in
  `gl-viewport.ts` by projecting the smoothed hero position with the live
  camera, and pass them as uniforms.
- **Numbers to start from:** screen radius **1.4 × the avatar's on-screen
  height** in pixels (so it scales with zoom), fade band 35 % of that, and a
  small depth bias (**0.15 cells**) so the hero's own cell does not flicker.
  Derive the avatar's on-screen height from the camera and distance rather
  than hard-coding pixels.
- Keep the avatar material exemption (`cutout: false`) — belt and braces.
- `web/src/gpu/cutout.ts`'s pure helpers should move with the rule: replace
  `isCutCell` with something that expresses the new test at cell resolution
  (a cell is cut when it is nearer the camera than the hero **and** projects
  within the screen radius), so it stays testable in node.
