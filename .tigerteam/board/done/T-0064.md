## Goal
The user, looking at the third-person view: *"can we make the player avatar
slightly less flat pls"*. The model is well detailed from the front but is a
slab in profile — the torso is 6.0 units wide and only 3.8 deep, so from the
diorama camera's 42° angle it reads as cardboard.

## Context
- **The sources are vendored in-repo at `vendor/afterburn/` (read-only).**
  `vendor/afterburn/src/models/pilot.js` is the reference — compare its
  torso/head/limb **depth-to-width ratios** against ours and note the
  difference in the docs.
- Current hero (`buildHeroModel`, builder units): tunic
  `6.0 w × 7.2 h × 3.8 d`, belt `6.4 × 1.3 × 4.0`, shoulder pad
  `1.9 × 1.5 × 3.2`, upper arm `1.7 × 3.4 × 3.0`, pant leg `2.6 × 11.0 × 3.0`,
  boot `2.9 × 2.3 × 3.9`.
- **The change, decided.** Bring the body's depth to roughly **0.8 × its
  width** instead of today's ~0.63, and round the silhouette so it is not a
  box:
  - torso depth `3.8 → 4.8`, belt `4.0 → 5.0`, head depth to match the torso
    within ~0.5;
  - add **chamfer boxes** at the torso's four vertical corners (a smaller
    box set in by ~0.6 on both horizontal axes, full height) so the chest
    reads as rounded rather than square — this is the trick
    `ART_BIBLE.md` §2 means by "curves are stair-stepped";
  - give the shoulders a little more depth than the chest (`+0.4`) so the
    figure has a silhouette from the side;
  - legs and arms up by the same ~25 % in depth.
- **Keep** the height at `HERO_SPRITE_HEIGHT` (0.7 cells) — this is depth
  only, the figure must not get taller or wider — and keep the box budget
  (≤ 400). Say the new box count in the docs.
- The pet may get the same treatment if it is cheap; if you leave it, say so.

### For the reviewer

- **The chamfer interpretation is a judgment call.** The ticket says
  "a smaller box set in by ~0.6 on both horizontal axes, full height"
  at each corner, so the chest reads as rounded. I read that as: at
  each vertical corner of the tunic, a 1.2 × 1.2 × 7.2 column whose
  inner face is 0.6 inside the tunic corner and whose outer face is
  0.6 beyond it on both x and z (i.e. it straddles the corner). From
  above this turns a hard 6.0 × 4.8 rectangle into a rectangle with a
  small stair-stepped extension at every corner — the trick
  `ART_BIBLE.md` §2 calls "stair-stepped curves". A stricter reading
  (chamfers entirely inside the tunic footprint) would be invisible,
  since `voxel/mesh.ts` gives every box six faces with no hidden-face
  culling between overlapping boxes; my reading is what actually
  produces the "rounded rather than square" read the ticket asks for.
- **The cloak/pack shifted 0.7 back.** Not called out in scope, but
  necessary: extending the tunic depth to 4.8 forward-only (keeping
  its back at z = −1.9) plus the back-corner chamfers protruding 0.6
  further back to z = −2.5 would otherwise poke through the cloak
  lining at z ∈ [−2.15, −1.85]. Post-shift the closest gap between a
  chamfer and the cloak lining is 0.05 units. If the reviewer prefers
  the cloak stay put, the fix is to drop the back-corner chamfers
  (which then makes the ticket's "four vertical corners" a two-corner
  chamfer, so I chose the shift).
- **Shoulder ratio overshoots the pilot on purpose.** Pilot shoulder
  cap is 2.8 w × 3.1 d (1.11 depth/width); the ticket asks for
  `chest + 0.4 = 5.2` depth on a 1.9-wide shoulder, i.e. 2.74. I
  followed the ticket verbatim; the resulting shoulder is a very
  strong silhouette from the side. If it looks pinched next to the
  head, that's a design pass, not a bug.
- **Visual verification is on the PM.** I have no display and no
  Playwright inside the worker container; structural verification is
  the box-count, height-preservation and 0.75 depth/width tests, which
  are green. Whether the avatar reads as an adventurer with real body
  from the 42° diorama camera is a screenshot the reviewer will need
  to eyeball.
