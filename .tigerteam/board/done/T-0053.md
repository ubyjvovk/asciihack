## Goal
The dungeon looks rained on. Afterburn's world is a planet in a storm, so its
moods carry heavy `wetness`/`puddles`, and the port inherited them: `torchlit`
sits at `wetness 0.5, puddles 0.25` and even `deep_dark` at `0.4 / 0.10`.
A dungeon is **mostly dry stone**. Make dry the default and wet the
exception, justified by what is actually in the level.

## Context
- **The sources are vendored in-repo at `vendor/afterburn/` (read-only).**
  `~/afterburn` is not visible inside the worker container.
- Read `docs/gpu-materials.md` (the `W` block and the mood table) and
  `docs/gpu-dungeon.md` (the kind → geometry table). In
  `web/src/gpu/materials.ts`, wetness darkens albedo and drops roughness, and
  puddles paint standing water on up-facing `ground` faces.
- **The mechanism is already there and unused.** `createVoxelMaterial` reads
  a per-box `FLAG_DRY` bit (`dry: true`) that exempts a surface from wetness
  and puddles entirely. `dungeon.ts` currently sets it only on emitters
  (lava, ice, torch flames). That flag is how "unless there's a reason" gets
  expressed per cell, rather than by turning the global knob down and making
  even genuinely wet places look dry.
- **Two changes, both decided:**
  1. **Moods** (`moods.ts`) — new `weather` values:
     | mood | wetness | puddles |
     |---|---|---|
     | `torchlit` | 0.5 → **0.10** | 0.25 → **0.0** |
     | `deep_dark` | 0.4 → **0.06** | 0.10 → **0.0** |
     | `ice` | 0.3 → **0.10** | 0.10 → **0.0** |
     | `lava` | 0.0 | 0.0 (unchanged — already dry) |
     | `flooded` | 1.0 | 1.0 (unchanged — that mood *is* the reason) |
     The small residual keeps a hint of sheen on stone under torchlight
     rather than a bone-dry matte; it is not meant to read as wet.
  2. **Per-cell damp** (`dungeon.ts`) — mark every stone box `dry: true`
     **except** where the level gives a reason: a cell that is `water`,
     `fountain`, `drawbridge` or `ice`, or is orthogonally adjacent to one,
     keeps the flag off so it still takes the mood's wetness and puddles.
     Export the pure predicate `isDampCell(level, x, y): boolean` and test it.
     Corridors, ordinary rooms, doors and stairs are dry.
- Net effect: in `flooded`, or beside a fountain, stone still goes properly
  wet and SSR still has something to reflect. Everywhere else it is rock.

### Notes for the reviewer
- **Ceilings.** The ticket's per-cell wording is "mark every stone box
  `dry: true`", and ceilings are stone `basalt0` `rock` slabs. I applied
  the same rule to `bakeChunkCeilingBoxes` and `bakeCeiling` — a ceiling
  above a fountain cell remains wet-eligible, everywhere else it goes dry.
  Flag if a reviewer prefers ceilings to be dry unconditionally; the change
  is one guarded loop in each helper.
- **`buildLava` and `buildIce`.** Both set `dry: true` on their emitter box
  through the material literal. Lava cells are not damp (unchanged mood,
  and lava is not in the ticket's damp-source list), so the loop runs and
  reasserts `dry: true` — a no-op on the flag byte. Ice cells *are* damp,
  so the loop is skipped and the ice slab keeps its material-level
  `dry: true`. Water/fountain/drawbridge cells are damp too — their
  stone (fountain basin/slabs, bridge span, water's own surface) stays
  wet-eligible so `flooded` still soaks and SSR still reflects.
- **What I could not verify.** Every claim about how the frame *looks*
  needs the PM's eye through `/scene.html`. The unit tests pin the pure
  rules (mood ranges, `isDampCell` topology, byte-identity, box budget,
  incremental cost) but no worker container can judge whether "0.10
  residual sheen" reads as "hint of shine" or "fully dry" in the amber
  ASCII output. The intent per the ticket is "not meant to read as wet";
  a follow-up tuning ticket may need to nudge the residuals (0.06..0.10)
  either up or to a hard 0.
