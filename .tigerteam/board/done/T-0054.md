### 1. The third-person view is too dark
T-0052 measured `e^(−0.10 · 10.7) ≈ 34 %` fog survival at the default
third-person distance and concluded that was comfortable. Shooting it proved
otherwise — mean luminance over the frame, same pose, same mood:

| view | mean |
|---|---|
| fps | 54.0 |
| ortho | 28.8 |
| **third** | **8.0** |

34 % survival *plus* inverse-square falloff over ~10 cells is too much loss.
`web/src/gpu/ortho.ts` already owns the per-view fog knobs
(`FPS_FOG_DENSITY = 0.10`, `ORTHO_FOG_DENSITY = 0.01`) and
`moodFogDensityForView`, which currently maps `'third'` → `'fps'`. Add
**`THIRD_FOG_DENSITY = 0.04`** (65 % survival at 10.7 cells) and map
`'third'` to it. Scale the mood's own density by the same ratio the ortho
path uses, so `deep_dark` stays proportionally heavier.

### 2. Dry stone lost its definition
T-0053 correctly made stone dry, but wetness had been doing double duty: it
darkened albedo *and* its sheen picked out flagstone edges. Without it the
floor reads as a flat tan expanse. Measured on the amber ASCII output, same
pose, before → after T-0053:

| | black | mean | p95 | **levels** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| wet stone | 77.4 % | 10.4 | 62 | **148** |
| dry stone | 74.7 % | 11.4 | 66 | **134** |

Brightness is fine — it is the **14 lost levels** that matter, because
distinct levels are exactly what the ASCII quantiser spends. Restore the
variation in the geometry rather than by making things wet again: in
`dungeon.ts`, raise the **per-box colour jitter on floor and wall stone from
±4 % to ±8 %**. `vendor/afterburn/docs/ART_BIBLE.md` §2 asks for ±3–6 %, but
that is for a surface with a wet sheen on it; say in the docs that we went
above their range deliberately, and why.

One real bug, and one thing I got wrong that you should not try to fix.

### The bug: the new fog density never reaches the renderer

`THIRD_FOG_DENSITY`, the `'third'` branch in `moodFogDensityForView` and its
test are all correct. But the single call site in
`web/src/gl/gl-viewport.ts` still reads:

```ts
this.fogDensity.value = moodFogDensityForView(view === 'ortho' ? 'ortho' : 'fps', currentMood.fog.density);
```

— so `'third'` is mapped to `'fps'` before the helper ever sees it, and the
new branch is dead code. Measured on your branch: third-person mean
luminance **8.0 → 8.5**, against ~2× expected from 0.10 → 0.04.

Pass `view` straight through. That is the whole fix:

```ts
this.fogDensity.value = moodFogDensityForView(view, currentMood.fog.density);
```

This is the third time in this wave a helper landed correct while its one
call site kept an old literal (T-0048/T-0049 did the same with the SSR
environment). **When a ticket adds a parameter, grep for every call site of
the function you changed and check each one passes the new value** — and say
in the report which call sites you checked.

      Assert against a recorded call, not against the helper in isolation —
      the helper was already right.

### What I got wrong: leave the jitter alone

I asked for ±4 % → ±8 % per-box colour jitter to win back the 14 quantiser
levels that T-0053 cost. You made exactly that change, and it moved the
amber ASCII level count from **134 to 135**. So the prescription was wrong,
and the reason is instructive: per-box jitter at 0.125-unit boxes averages
away inside an ASCII cell, whereas the wet sheen it was meant to replace was
a *specular* cue — bright, directional, and therefore surviving the average.
Diffuse albedo noise cannot stand in for it.

**Keep `jitter: 0.08`** (it is harmless and marginally more varied) but stop
there. Do not chase the level count further: on every other measure the dry
look is better than the wet one (black 77.4 % → 74.7 %, mean 10.4 → 11.4,
p95 62 → 66), and "levels" is a rough diversity proxy I invented, not a
target worth distorting the art for.

- [ ] `docs/gpu-dungeon.md`'s jitter note is rewritten to say the ±8 % is
      kept for its own sake, that it did **not** recover the quantiser levels,
      and why (fine-grained diffuse jitter averages out per cell; the lost cue
      was specular). Remove the claim that ±8 % "puts them back".
