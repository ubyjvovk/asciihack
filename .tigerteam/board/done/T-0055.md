## Goal
Two things the user called out on first look at the finished renderer. Both
are small and both are decided.

## Context
- **The sources are vendored in-repo at `vendor/afterburn/` (read-only).**
  `~/afterburn` is not visible inside the worker container.

### 1. Turn the particles off
T-0044 ported afterburn's weather overlay and gave the dungeon drifting dust
motes and drips. Seen in motion the user's verdict was *"stop the particles
all over, makes no sense in the dungeon"* — and they are right: afterburn's
motes belong to an open valley in the rain, not a sealed stone room.

**Keep the machinery, emit nothing by default.** Set the default particle
counts for **motes and drips to zero** so no emitter runs unless something
asks for it. Do not delete `weather.ts`, do not unwire the overlay pass from
the pipeline, and do not remove the mood plumbing — a later ticket may want
embers over a lava pool, and that should be a number change, not a rebuild.
Make the zero the *default*, with the existing counts reachable through the
same options the module already takes.

### 2. No completely black floor tiles
`buildFloor` in `dungeon.ts` splits a cell into four 4×1×4 flagstone
quadrants and **skips each with 10 % probability** (`b.chance(0.9)`),
leaving a hole that renders as a black square in the middle of a lit floor.
The user: *"avoid completely black tiles on the floor unless it is actually
a pit"*.

**Always place all four quadrants.** Express wear the way the rest of the
kit does — vary the *colour* and *height*, not presence: give a "worn"
quadrant (same 10 % chance, keep the seeded roll so the level still varies)
a **`basalt0` tile recessed by one voxel** (`y = -0.125`, height 1) instead
of omitting it. A sunken, darker flagstone reads as wear; a hole reads as a
bug. The cracked mud pebble can stay as it is.
