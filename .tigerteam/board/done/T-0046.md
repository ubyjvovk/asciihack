## Goal
`DungeonScene` rebakes the **whole level** whenever the cell-kind hash
changes. In NetHack that hash changes on almost every step while exploring —
each newly-seen cell is a new kind — so a 40 000-box, ~50 ms rebake lands on
the frame that draws the player's move. Bake in chunks and rebuild only the
chunks that actually changed.

## Context
- This runs **in parallel with T-0040**, which owns `web/src/gpu/path.ts`,
  `compose.ts`, `pipeline.ts`, `gl-viewport.ts` and `main.ts`. Touch none of
  them; this ticket lives entirely inside `dungeon.ts` and its test and doc.
- **The sources are vendored in-repo at `vendor/afterburn/` (read-only).**
  `~/afterburn` is not visible inside the worker container. Afterburn solves
  exactly this with `bakeModel(writer, model, matrix)` baking into **world
  chunks** — read `vendor/afterburn/src/voxel/mesh.js` and
  `vendor/afterburn/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §1 before designing anything.
- Read `docs/gpu-dungeon.md` (the current bake, its box budget and its
  measured timings) and `docs/gpu.md` §8 (the frame budget).
- **The design, decided:** split the level into fixed chunks of **10 × 7
  cells** (an 80 × 21 level is then 8 × 3 = 24 chunks), each its own
  `GeoWriter` → `BufferGeometry` → `Mesh` under `DungeonScene.root`. Keep a
  per-chunk hash of its cells' kinds and `lit` flags; on `refresh`, rehash
  every chunk and rebake only those whose hash moved. Dispose the old
  geometry of a rebuilt chunk. The `ceiling` group is chunked the same way so
  it stays separable.
- Per-chunk hashing must be cheaper than baking — hash the kind and `lit` of
  the chunk's cells only, not the whole level, and keep the level-wide hash as
  a fast "nothing changed at all" early-out.
- Determinism is unchanged: jitter is still seeded from absolute cell
  coordinates, so a cell bakes identically whichever chunk it lands in and
  chunk borders are invisible.
- Lights and the torch cap stay level-wide (`updateLights` is unchanged) —
  only geometry is chunked.

### Reviewer note — the one deliberate correctness tradeoff
`DungeonScene.refresh` recomputes hidden-face masks only for chunks whose own boxes changed, not for their axial neighbours. An always-4-neighbours re-cull matches the full-bake exactly at every step but pushed the single-cell reveal to ~10 ms, doubling the ticket's 5 ms budget. Skipping the neighbour pass leaves only one kind of geometric artifact: a face on my neighbour that toggles visibility as a wall grows or vanishes exactly on our shared chunk boundary. In every such case the extra (or missing) face sits inside the new / former wall body and cannot be seen from a walkable pose — the wall's own outward face covers it. The initial full bake (`bakeChunks`, always called after a `_reshape`) uses correct halo culling for every chunk, so the byte-identity property is unchanged, and the "byte-identical" test verifies that from scratch. This is documented in `docs/gpu-dungeon.md` under "Correctness tradeoff at chunk boundaries" and in "What I could not verify".
