## Goal
Turn a `LevelView` into the voxel geometry the ported renderer lights: stone
walls of stacked mixed-size blocks, flagstone floors with cracks and missing
tiles, doorways with posts and a lintel, plank doors, stepped stairs, and
emissive torch sconces with real point lights. This replaces the textured
unit cubes of `web/src/gl/scene-builder.ts` for the GPU path — that file
stays untouched as the fallback (`docs/gpu.md` §7).

## Context
- **The sources are vendored in-repo at `vendor/afterburn/` (read-only).**
  `~/afterburn` itself is **not** visible inside the worker container —
  read the vendored copy, and diff your port against it before you hand
  off. A port written from a description instead of the source is not a
  port (this cost T-0037 an attempt).
- **Read first**: `docs/gpu.md` §4 (coordinates, cell = 1 unit, voxel unit =
  0.125) and §5, `docs/gpu-voxel.md` (landed by T-0036), and
  `vendor/afterburn/docs/ART_BIBLE.md` §2 "Voxel rules" — mixed box sizes, per-box
  colour jitter, broken silhouettes, tiny meaningful emissives. Then look at
  how `web/src/gl/scene-builder.ts` reads a level (the kind table, the
  `door_axis` inference, the `hashKinds` rebuild gate) and keep those
  behaviours.
- `LevelView` (`src/model/types.ts`) gives `width`, `height`,
  `kindAt(x, y)`, `cellAt(x, y)` (→ `MapCell | null`, carrying `lit`). A cell
  with `kindAt === 'unexplored'` is **not known**: it contributes no geometry
  at all — the darkness is the fog's job, exactly as today.
- **Art direction — this ticket is where the dungeon either looks like
  afterburn or does not.** `vendor/afterburn/docs/ART_BIBLE.md` §2 is the
  rulebook: mixed box sizes (big calm slabs next to clusters of tiny
  detail), **every surface gets ±3–6 % per-box colour jitter, never a flat
  fill**, broken silhouettes (chipped corners, missing tiles, stacked
  offsets, overgrowth), no perfect symmetry, and **emissive bits stay tiny
  and meaningful** (a torch head, never a glowing slab). Use the palette keys
  from `web/src/voxel/palette.ts`: walls `basalt1`/`basalt2` with `slate0`
  chips and `moss0`/`lichen` in the damp corners, floors `basalt0`/`basalt1`
  flagstones with `mud` in the cracks, doors `wood0`/`wood1` with `brass`
  hinges, stairs `slate1`, torches `fire` + `ember`, water `water`, lava
  `ember`, ice `crystalCold0`/`crystalCold1`. `vendor/afterburn/src/models/_demo.js`
  is the canonical example of how a model is written with the kit — read it
  before your first `b.box(...)`.
- Build with the kit from `web/src/voxel/`: a `GeoWriter` per rebuild, one
  merged `BufferGeometry` for the whole known level, plus a separate list of
  point lights. Use `bakeModel`/`hiddenFaces` so interior faces are culled.
- **Kind → geometry table (implement these; anything else falls back to the
  `floor` treatment, and `unexplored` to nothing):**
  | kind | geometry |
  |---|---|
  | `wall`, `stone`, and any other solid | stone blocks filling the cell, mixed sizes, chipped top corners, ±4 % colour jitter, `stone` flag set |
  | `floor` | flagstones at `y = 0`, a few cracked or missing, `ground` flag set |
  | `corridor` | rougher rock floor, no flagstone seams |
  | `doorway`, `door_open` | floor + two vertical posts + a lintel across the top |
  | `door_closed` | posts + lintel + a plank door slab with a hinge and a handle, axis inferred from the neighbouring walls |
  | `stairs_up`, `stairs_down` | four stepped boxes climbing/descending, plus a faint emissive nosing |
  | `water` | flat surface at `y ≈ 0.1`, low roughness, high metalness (SSR does the rest) |
  | `lava` | flat surface, emissive, `fx = pulse` |
  | `ice` | flat surface, low roughness, pale |
  | `fountain`, `altar`, `grave`, `sink`, `throne` | a small distinctive prop on a floor cell — keep each ≤ 40 boxes |
- **Ceiling — decided.** The legacy scene has none: walls are cubes and the
  space above them is open, so torchlight escapes upward and there is nothing
  for the SSGI bounce to come off. Add a ceiling over every **known passable**
  cell at `y = 1` (the top of the wall cubes), as stone matching the walls but
  a shade darker, built into a **separate `Object3D` group named `ceiling`**
  on `DungeonScene.root`. The first-person path shows it; the ortho path (a
  later ticket) hides that group, which is why it must be separable rather
  than merged into the main geometry. Sprites are at most 1.3 cells tall and
  will clip it — that is accepted, note it in the docs.
- **Torches.** Place a sconce on a wall cell that borders a lit floor cell
  (`cellAt(...).lit === true`), at most one per 6 cells along a wall, each an
  emissive box with `fx = flicker` plus a `THREE.PointLight`. **Cap the
  number of live point lights at 8**, keeping the ones nearest the hero;
  expose `updateLights(heroX, heroY)` so the choice follows the hero without
  rebuilding geometry.
- **Shadows — decided, do not deviate.** Torch shadows are the dungeon look,
  but a cube shadow map per point light is six extra passes each, and eight
  of them would blow the budget. So: the merged level mesh gets
  `castShadow = true` **and** `receiveShadow = true`, and `updateLights`
  marks only the **two torches nearest the hero** as `castShadow = true`,
  clearing it on the rest as they fall out of range. Everything else is lit
  flat and gets its contact darkening from the SSGI/AO term. Put the rule and
  the measured cost in `docs/gpu-dungeon.md`.
- **Budgets (`docs/gpu.md` §8).** ≤ 12 boxes per cell on average, ≤ 40 000
  boxes for a fully-known 80×21 level, and a rebuild under 50 ms. Rebuild only
  when the level's kind-hash changes, like `SceneBuilder.refresh`.
- Determinism: seed every jitter from the cell coordinates, so the same level
  bakes identically twice and nothing shimmers between frames.
- Public API (keep it small): `export class DungeonScene` with
  `readonly root: THREE.Object3D`, `refresh(level): boolean`,
  `updateLights(x, y): void`, `dispose(): void`, and an exported pure
  `bakeLevel(level, opts)` that returns `{ writer, lights, boxCount }` so the
  tests can run it without a renderer.
- TS strict, named exports, no `any`, relative imports end in `.js`, one-line
  JSDoc per export. `tests/gpu-dungeon.test.ts` runs in node under the root
  tsconfig: import `bakeLevel` and the kit, never a renderer.
- **`tests/fixtures/levels.ts` needs two backward-compatible additions** (it
  is in scope for exactly these, and nothing else — every existing test must
  keep passing unchanged):
  1. `charToKind` gains `L` → `lava`, `I` → `ice`, `{` → `fountain`. Do not
     change or repurpose any existing glyph.
  2. `levelFromAscii(rows, opts?)` gains an optional second argument
     `{ lit?: (kind: CellKind, x: number, y: number) => boolean | undefined }`
     whose result is put on the `MapCell.lit` that `cellAt` returns. Absent,
     `lit` stays `undefined` exactly as today. Without this the torch rule
     (which keys off `cellAt(...).lit === true`) cannot be tested at all.
  Update the `Legend:` JSDoc line to match.

### Look closely at

- **Torch spacing rule.** I interpreted "at most one per 6 cells along a
  wall" as a **global Chebyshev spacing of 6 cells between chosen torches**,
  applied greedily in row-major order after collecting one candidate side
  per wall cell. That reliably keeps torch counts sane on big rooms
  (32 on the 80×21 test level, of which `updateLights` keeps the nearest 8),
  but if the PM meant "6 cells along each single wall run in isolation" the
  spacing could be slightly denser at wall junctions. Trivial to switch —
  it is one function, `collectTorches`.
- **Box centroid inclusion test.** `tests/gpu-dungeon.test.ts` verifies that
  each explored cell contributes at least one vertex whose XZ position is
  **strictly inside** the cell interior (open interval). That works because
  the wall corner caps, floor tiles and doorway posts all leave interior
  vertices — but a level fixture that trims those (e.g. removes corner caps
  entirely) would need a different inclusion probe.
- **What I could not verify.** Every look claim in the docs is behind that
  section, on purpose. Please eyeball `/scene.html?render=amber` and
  `?gpu=raw&q=high` against `.tigerteam/shots/before-amber.png` and send a
  tuning ticket if the torches, ceiling, or props need adjusting — the
  budget is nowhere near tight (4.03 boxes/cell average vs the 12 cap), so
  there is room to grow prop detail.
